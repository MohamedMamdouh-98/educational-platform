Example in Mathematics Teaching: Quadratic Functions

Digital Tools

PowerPoint, GeoGebra, Google Docs, Google Meet (Breakout Rooms), and Google Forms.

Steps for Implementing the Digital Discovery Learning Strategy

  1. 1 Introduction

    The teacher presents the concept of quadratic functions using PowerPoint and asks learners how changing the coefficients of a quadratic function affects the shape and position of its graph.

  2. 2 Exploration

    The teacher asks learners to use GeoGebra to graph and compare the following functions: g(x) = 2x², f(x) = x², h(x) = (x − 2)².

  3. 3 Data Collection

    Learners record their observations in Google Docs, noting that g(x) = 2x² is narrower than f(x) = x² while h(x) = (x − 2)² is shifted two units to the right.

  4. 4 Discovery

    Based on their observations, learners discover that changing the coefficient of (x²) affects the width of the parabola, while changing the expression inside the square affects its horizontal position.

  5. 5 Discussion

    The teacher divides learners into small groups using the Breakout Rooms feature in Google Meet. Each group discusses its findings, explains the observed relationships, and predicts how other changes in a quadratic function would affect its graph.

  6. 6 Evaluation

    The teacher uses Google Forms to assess learners’ understanding by asking them to predict the graph of a new function, such as y = (x + 3)².
    Learners should identify that its graph is shifted three units to the left compared with (y = x²).
